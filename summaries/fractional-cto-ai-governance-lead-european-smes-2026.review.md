## 500-word summary

Many European professional services firms and founder-led companies in 2026 have deployed multiple AI tools without anyone owning AI governance. A 25-person company using Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude, and a sector-specific niche vendor tool faces regulatory, vendor, and operational exposure with no clear ownership for policy, incident response, or vendor agreement review. This governance gap is the core problem a fractional CTO engagement addresses. The EU AI Act has been enforceable since August 2024 for prohibited practices and applies to high-risk categories from August 2026, requiring documented risk classification for any AI tool falling under Annex III categories such as recruitment screening, credit scoring, critical infrastructure management, or biometric categorisation. Vendor risk arises when AI tool vendors operate under US data residency defaults without valid EU data transfer mechanisms for personal data of EU residents. Operational risk materialises when AI tools produce incorrect outputs used in client deliverables without an established incident response process. A typical fractional CTO engagement runs one to two days per week and covers five core areas: AI tool inventory and risk classification mapping tools against EU AI Act categories and GDPR obligations; AI use policy drafting covering approved tools, decision types requiring human review, and prohibited uses; vendor assessment reviewing DPAs, data residency configurations, and subprocessor chains; incident response setup defining what constitutes an incident, reporting chains, and escalation paths; and quarterly governance reviews updating inventories and policies as the regulatory landscape evolves. Internal teams retain responsibility for day-to-day prompt management, tool selection for specific use cases, employee AI literacy training, and frontline incident reporting. The fractional model has clear boundaries: it does not provide sector-specific domain expertise such as veterinary practice standards or financial advice regulations, and it does not provide on-call availability for incident response. Cost analysis shows fractional CTO governance at €2,000-€3,500 per month, internal AI Operations Manager hiring at €25,000-€35,000 annually with recruitment and management overhead, and one-time consulting audits at €15,000-€25,000 producing reports without ongoing ownership. The fractional model is more cost-efficient than internal ownership for companies below 80-100 employees where the governance workload does not justify a full-time role and where senior part-time talent is rarely available in European labour markets. The crossover point for internal ownership typically arrives around 80-100 employees when the AI tool portfolio and compliance surface become complex enough to justify dedicated internal leadership. Key FAQ points confirm that fractional CTOs cannot certify EU AI Act compliance themselves (they prepare organisations for third-party assessment), that fractional implies ongoing ownership versus project-scoped consulting, and that initial governance deliverables typically materialise within four to six weeks with vendor DPA reviews adding another two to four weeks.
