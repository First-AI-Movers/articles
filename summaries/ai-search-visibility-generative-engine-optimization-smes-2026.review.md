## 500-word summary

European SMEs face a new visibility challenge: AI search tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity return synthesised answers that mention specific businesses, but the evidence base differs fundamentally from Google rankings. A firm that ranks on page one for keywords may be invisible in AI answers if the only content about the business is its own website. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) addresses this gap by structuring content, authority signals, and online presence to surface in AI-generated responses.

The AI evidence base comes from four sources: pre-cutoff training data, real-time web browsing for enabled models, structured sources like Wikipedia and LinkedIn, and content appearing prominently across multiple independent sources. For a 15-person professional services firm, ranking well on Google but having no third-party mentions creates a specific vulnerability—AI models need cross-source confirmation before mentioning a business confidently.

The article outlines five practical GEO steps for SME teams without dedicated marketing functions. Step one involves building cross-source presence through industry directories with consistent naming, earning mentions in local or industry publications, and ensuring complete LinkedIn company profiles. A practical example for a 12-person fintech consultancy in Dublin includes submitting to three Irish tech directories, responding to journalist queries via Qwoted or Help a Reporter Out, and maintaining active LinkedIn presence—an hour or two monthly.

Step two focuses on publishing answers to specific buyer questions rather than generic service descriptions. A page stating "We help European SMEs implement AI strategy" will not surface for "How do European SMEs choose an AI strategy consultant?" Instead, content directly answering questions like typical engagement costs, sector experience requirements, and assessment timelines mirrors AI response formats and increases citation probability.

Step three recommends implementing schema markup (LocalBusiness, Organization, Person, Service, FAQPage) using CMS plugins or built-in settings. This provides machine-readable signals AI models can reference without inferring from unstructured text.

Step four establishes team professional footprints because AI models often reference individuals when answering expertise questions. Complete LinkedIn profiles, quarterly substantive articles, and event speaking credits build the evidence base for confident firm recommendations.

Step five involves monitoring through monthly targeted queries across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, tracking when the firm starts appearing. Perplexity's inline citations provide particularly useful feedback on which content works.

Common mistakes include publishing low-quality AI-generated content (penalized by models), focusing only on own website instead of cross-source presence, and expecting fast results. AI training cycles mean newly created content may not influence answers for weeks or months, requiring a six-to-twelve-month horizon. The minimum viable GEO programme—consistent directory listings, one Q&A page per service, basic schema markup, and quarterly monitoring—represents four to six hours setup plus one to two hours monthly monitoring.
