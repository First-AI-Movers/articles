## 500-word summary

This article presents a pragmatic 90-day AI transformation roadmap designed specifically for mid-market companies, addressing the reality that while AI adoption is accelerating—particularly among Dutch companies with 50 to 250 employees, which have seen adoption rise from 20% to 45%—the execution path remains unclear for many organizations. The core argument is that mid-market teams do not need a grand AI strategy deck but rather a structured roadmap that creates real business value while avoiding disconnected pilots and analysis paralysis. The roadmap is built on three essential layers that must be developed simultaneously: first, picking one or two high-value use cases where the pain is real, the owner is clear, the process repeats often, data is accessible, and value becomes visible within weeks; second, building the adoption path at the same time as development rather than treating rollout as a post-build activity—this includes ensuring managers know workflow ownership, teams trust outputs, training exists, and human override capabilities are defined; and third, establishing minimum viable governance that answers basic questions about allowed tools, approved use cases, required human oversight, sensitive data handling, output review processes, vendor decision sign-offs, and incident ownership. The article provides a detailed implementation timeline across the ninety days: days one through fifteen focus on diagnosing and choosing with a shortlist of three to five use cases, one clear first use case, executive sponsor, workflow owner, and success metrics; days sixteen through forty-five emphasize building one controlled, high-value workflow improvement with narrow scope, clear inputs and outputs, human review points, and measurable before-and-after comparison—specifically warning against building platforms, launching multiple experiments, or trying to transform every department at once; days forty-six through seventy-five involve training, refining, and instrumenting to answer whether people are actually using the system, what they are overriding, where outputs break, and what the real savings or speed gains are; and days seventy-six through ninety require leadership decisions about what scales next, what pauses, what capabilities need strengthening, and what operating model is needed. The article explicitly advises delaying broad internal AI platforms, multi-department expansion, heavy custom engineering, full autonomy claims, and massive governance machinery until evidence from the first ninety days exists. It identifies five patterns to avoid completely: tool-led strategy where tools are bought before workflows are defined, innovation theater that produces excitement but no owner or commercial outcome, technical isolation where engineering defines value without business ownership, adoption blindness that assumes rollout equals usage, and compliance as an afterthought addressed only after deployment. The article concludes that most mid-market companies do not need a full AI transformation office on day one but rather one roadmap, one first use case, one accountable owner, one adoption path, one governance baseline, and one decision point at day ninety—emphasizing that the winners in the coming period will be those who turn one workflow into one measurable business result and then repeat the process with discipline.
