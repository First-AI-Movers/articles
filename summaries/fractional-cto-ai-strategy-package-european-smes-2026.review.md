## 500-word summary

Fractional CTO AI engagements provide European scale-ups (typically 15-50 employees) with senior technical leadership at a fraction of a full-time salary, structured around defined scopes and deliverables. This model addresses a critical gap: founder-led companies and professional services firms are making AI vendor decisions now that will shape their compliance posture under the EU AI Act for years to come, yet most cannot justify a full-time CTO. Three engagement models serve different organizational stages. The AI Readiness Review (one to two weeks, €2,500-€4,000 fixed fee) serves as an entry point for companies that have prioritized AI but need an independent audit before committing to investment, producing a ranked opportunity list and compliance gap summary. The AI Strategy Package (three months, €2,000-€3,500/month, €6,000-€10,500 total) is the most common engagement, building the roadmap, leading vendor selection, supervising initial implementation, and defining the training framework for internal teams—delivering an operational AI stack or staged implementation plan with named internal owners. The Ongoing Advisory model (six to twelve months, €1,500-€2,500/month) serves firms with live AI stacks requiring quarterly strategy reviews, model evaluation as the market evolves, budget governance, and continuous team upskilling. Regardless of scope, the first 30 days follow the same pattern: two weeks of diagnostic work auditing tools, mapping data governance baselines and sensitive data locations, and identifying quick-win automation candidates; followed by two weeks of prioritization presenting findings to leadership, agreeing on a 90-day target state, and identifying an internal champion who will own the AI agenda after the engagement ends. A concrete example: a 28-person professional services firm in Amsterdam engaged a fractional CTO for a three-month AI Strategy Package, resulting in three automated reporting processes using OpenAI's API, a vendor shortlist with confirmed DPAs, an AI acceptable-use policy, and a trained internal champion (the operations director) who could evaluate new tools independently—all for €8,500. Four critical questions should be answered before signing: who owns the IP of any tools built during the engagement (unambiguously the client company); what knowledge transfer and documentation will be provided; who is the internal champion (without one, the engagement produces a report that sits in a folder); and how will GDPR compliance be handled for any new AI vendors introduced. A fractional CTO is not the right answer when daily technical leadership is needed for an active product build (requiring a full-time or contract CTO), or when the AI use case is simple enough to handle with a single SaaS subscription where the advisory overhead is unjustified. For European companies, every fractional CTO scope should include GDPR compliance review (ensuring data processing agreements exist for every AI vendor processing personal data) and EU AI Act classification (applying the risk framework to AI systems used in HR, credit decisions, or client-facing processes).
