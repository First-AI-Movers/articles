The article argues that while 88% of organizations use AI, 77% of implementations fail to deliver results—and this is fundamentally a consulting problem rather than a technology problem. Traditional consulting delivers what Costa describes as beautiful strategy documents that create minimal operational change. He illustrates this with a striking example: a CEO who spent $50,000 on consulting but couldn't implement any of the 47 identified opportunities. The traditional 8-12 week analysis-to-presentation model moves too slowly for rapidly evolving AI landscapes, and by the time strategies are delivered, the competitive window has often closed. This disconnect between strategy and execution represents a systemic failure in how organizations approach AI adoption, leaving many companies with expensive reports that gather dust while competitors move ahead with practical implementations.

Costa proposes a done-with-you alternative model with three distinct phases designed to bridge the gap between theory and practice. Weeks 1-2 focus on immediate implementation, prioritizing quick wins such as automating lead qualification, which can save 40 hours monthly and demonstrate tangible value early in the engagement. Weeks 3-6 shift to system building, where teams actively construct AI infrastructure rather than passively observing consultants at work. This hands-on approach ensures that internal teams develop the skills necessary to maintain and extend AI capabilities over time. Weeks 7-10 emphasize knowledge transfer through comprehensive documentation and structured training sessions designed to ensure internal capability ownership after the engagement concludes.

The article identifies three essential systems for AI success that organizations must develop: a unified operations platform connecting disconnected business tools to create a single source of truth, a content engine that transforms single assets into multiple formats to maximize ROI on content creation, and custom AI assistants designed for business-specific intelligent systems tailored to particular workflow requirements. These three systems form the foundation of a sustainable AI infrastructure that can grow with organizational needs.

Success criteria for AI consulting engagements include proof of working implementations rather than just case studies or slides, technical expertise including coding ability and API/architecture knowledge to build actual systems, a knowledge transfer focus that prioritizes teaching over doing, and realistic timelines that acknowledge the complexity of organizational change. The ROI framework contrasts costs compellingly: done-with-you consulting runs approximately $10,000, while AI engineer salaries exceed $140,000 annually or traditional enterprise consulting costs $50,000+ for strategy alone without implementation guarantees. Costa concludes with a call to action: start with one bottleneck process rather than waiting for perfect strategy, positioning this incremental approach as the method used by the successful 23% of organizations implementing AI. This pragmatic focus on execution over planning distinguishes organizations that achieve AI success from those that remain trapped in endless strategy cycles.
