Europe is mobilizing approximately €200 billion to boost AI development, including €20 billion to finance up to five AI gigafactories, with 19 AI factories intended to support startups, industry, and research. The leadership question has fundamentally changed: it is no longer sufficient to ask which AI tools the company should purchase, but rather how the business should be redesigned for a world where machine-generated work becomes cheaper, faster, and easier to deploy across functions. Nvidia is framing AI in terms of sovereign infrastructure and industrial capacity, with its sovereign AI message resonating with European leaders as they consider digital sovereignty and industrial competitiveness, while OpenAI is expanding its Europe agenda across policy areas including education, health, skills, cybersecurity, and startup accelerators while building platforms for enterprises to deploy and manage agents across the business. The companies that win will be those that connect these signals to an operating model, requiring a serious operating response across five fronts: strategy, economics, sovereignty, workflow design, and executive execution. Organizations that see the strongest results are much more likely to redesign workflows and define when human validation is required. The strongest companies in Europe will win not by copying Silicon Valley language or waiting for perfect regulatory certainty, but by reading the moment correctly and recognizing that AI is becoming infrastructure, token economics are becoming managerial, sovereignty is becoming operational, workflow design is becoming a leadership responsibility, and the CEO agenda is shifting from curiosity to execution.
