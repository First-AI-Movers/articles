## 500-word summary

This HealthTech pitch deck template provides a comprehensive 14-slide structure designed specifically for healthcare startups, synthesized from analysis of over 100 funded healthcare pitch decks from 2022-2025, healthcare pitch deck best practices, and the Sequoia Capital framework adapted for the healthcare industry. The template addresses the unique challenges of presenting HealthTech ventures to investors by incorporating healthcare-specific guidance throughout each slide category. The recommended slide sequence begins with a title and hook containing the company name, logo, one-line tagline, and contact information, followed by the problem slide that quantifies the healthcare pain point with hard data showing who is affected and the cost of the problem. The solution slide mirrors the problem structure and shows how the product directly addresses the identified pain point with key differentiators. The timing slide addresses market readiness through regulatory changes, technology maturity including AI and ML capabilities, post-pandemic behavior shifts, and demographic trends such as aging populations. The product demonstration slide shows user journeys and highlights integration capabilities with existing healthcare systems, with specific guidance to show EHR and workflow integration explicitly as this signals credibility to investors. Market sizing uses the TAM/SAM/SOM framework with healthcare-specific market data including the global healthcare market valued at $4.7 trillion with 5.4% CAGR, digital health at $659 billion with 25.1% CAGR, remote patient monitoring at $31.3 billion with 28.9% CAGR, and AI in healthcare attracting $10.5 billion in investment in 2024 alone. Traction and validation slides emphasize clinical outcomes data, hospital partnerships, and stage-appropriate metrics from advisory boards and pilot agreements at pre-seed through multi-site deployment and market share data at Series B and beyond. The business model slide requires explicit identification of who pays, addressing the complexity that healthcare buyers often differ from end users. The regulatory and clinical validation slide frames regulation as a competitive moat rather than a risk, with guidance on FDA pathways including 510(k), De Novo, and PMA processes, HIPAA compliance, and early engagement with AI governance processes. The multi-stakeholder value proposition slide presents value across providers, payers, and patients in a three-column format with metrics for each stakeholder group. The guide includes stage-specific recommendations where pre-seed companies should use 10 slides focusing on vision and team with typical raises of $1-3 million, seed stage uses 12 slides emphasizing problem and early traction for $2-5 million raises, Series A employs 14 slides leading with traction and unit economics for $5-20 million raises, Series B expands to 16 slides focused on scale and market dominance for $20-100 million raises, and Series C+ uses 18-25 slides emphasizing market leadership and financials for $100 million or larger raises. Common mistakes identified include ignoring regulatory strategy, claiming no competitors, leading with technology instead of outcomes, failing to specify payment sources, overclaiming about replacing clinicians, lacking clinical evidence, and creating dense text slides that overwhelm investors who typically spend only three to four minutes reviewing each deck.
