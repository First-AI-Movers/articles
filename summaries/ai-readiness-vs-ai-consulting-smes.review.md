## 500-word summary

Understanding the difference between AI readiness and AI consulting is crucial for businesses looking to adopt AI effectively. These two paths are related but solve different problems, and choosing the wrong starting point can slow progress and create avoidable waste. An AI readiness assessment helps leadership understand whether the business, teams, workflows, and operating conditions are ready for AI adoption. It evaluates current-state gaps, operating risk, and what needs to change before scaling. In contrast, AI consulting helps leadership decide where to focus, what to prioritize, and what the next practical move should be. It provides sharper business priorities, clearer ownership and sequencing, and a practical path forward. Leaders should start with readiness work when the business is still operationally uncertain. This is the better first move when teams are already experimenting without common standards, when leadership does not trust current workflows, controls, or ownership structures, when the business lacks a clear view of operating risk, or when executives want a grounded baseline before committing time or budget. A readiness assessment gives leadership a better foundation for action in these situations. Leaders should start with consulting when the business is strategically uncertain. This is the better first move when leaders already see likely use cases, when the main problem is prioritization rather than diagnosis, when the business needs help sequencing decisions, or when executives want an external view before committing resources. Effective AI strategy consulting helps narrow choices and clarify the path forward. The simple rule of thumb is: choose readiness if the business is operationally uncertain, which sounds like we are not sure our workflows, controls, or ownership are ready. Choose consulting if the business is strategically uncertain, which sounds like we know AI matters but we need help deciding where to focus. Some companies need both readiness and consulting. This is common when leadership wants to move quickly but the operating foundation is still weak. In that case, the best sequence is often to run a focused readiness assessment first, use the findings to narrow the consulting scope, then move forward with clearer priorities and lower risk. This sequence is usually faster than starting with a broad advisory engagement. From readiness work, leaders should receive a clear view of current-state gaps, a view of operating risk, and guidance on what should change before scale. From consulting, leaders should receive sharper business priorities, clearer ownership and sequencing, and a practical path forward. If an offer cannot explain those outputs clearly, it will be difficult for leaders to make good decisions about which path to pursue. The distinction matters because AI adoption without the right foundation wastes budget, creates frustration, and can damage credibility with stakeholders. Getting the sequence right positions organizations to move faster once they commit to implementation.
