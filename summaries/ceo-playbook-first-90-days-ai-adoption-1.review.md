## 500-word summary

The CEO Playbook for the First 90 Days of AI Adoption argues that the chief executive's primary role in early AI adoption is to create alignment and discipline, not a pile of disconnected experiments. The article, by Dr. Hernani Costa of First AI Movers, outlines a phased approach broken into three 30-day periods and stresses that many weak AI programs fail when curiosity is mistaken for strategy and activity for progress.

Days 1 to 30 focus on defining the business case. CEOs should narrow the problem by identifying where the business experiences repetitive friction, which decisions are slow or inconsistent, whether AI is the right lever, and who should own the initiative. The key admonition is not to start by buying tools but by defining which business problem is worth solving first.

Days 31 to 60 involve assessing readiness and constraints. Once the business case is clearer, leadership should pressure-test operating conditions: workflow stability, data and system constraints, governance expectations, leadership bandwidth, and review or escalation paths. At this stage, some companies should move into an AI Readiness Assessment instead of rushing into pilots.

Days 61 to 90 are about choosing a narrow first move. The goal is not scale but a controlled first move. Options include a tightly scoped workflow experiment, a consulting engagement to sharpen priorities, targeted team training tied to one workflow change, or a decision to wait until readiness improves. All four can be correct; the mistake is pretending the company is ready for scale when it is not.

The CEO should personally own the reason for adoption, the ambition level and budget discipline, the decision-maker role when trade-offs appear, and the standard for success. The CEO does not need to manage every technical detail but must remove ambiguity. The article lists what to avoid in the first 90 days: tool-first buying, unclear ownership between technology and operations, pilots with no decision criteria, innovation work disconnected from a business priority, and training that creates awareness without behavior change. These patterns create motion without leverage.

At the day-90 checkpoint, leadership should be able to answer five questions: What business problem are we solving first? Who owns the work? What is the next scoped move? What are the main risks? Do we need consulting, readiness work, or implementation support next? If these answers remain vague, the company should not scale yet. The article then suggests that if the checkpoint reveals more ambiguity than clarity, leaders should consider an AI Readiness Assessment to clarify operational state and surface hidden risks, or AI Consulting to refine strategic priorities and design the first high-impact move. The piece concludes with links to further reading and author credentials, noting Dr. Costa's background as an AI Architect and strategic advisor.
