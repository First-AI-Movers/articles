## 500-word summary

NN Group's artificial intelligence strategy exemplifies a thoughtful, human-centered approach that prioritizes strategic deployment over blanket adoption. Under the direction of Chief Analytics Officer Tjerrie Smit, the organization has defined its AI philosophy around a clear principle: deploy AI where it creates genuine value, not merely because the technology exists. This disciplined approach stands in contrast to organizations pursuing aggressive, enterprise-wide AI implementation without clear use-case rationale, and it reflects a belief that the most effective AI deployments are those with clearly defined business outcomes rather than technology-first experimentation.

The concept Smit uses internally to describe the customer experience is the "two-minute company" — a vision where individuals can manage their financial affairs quickly and conveniently at any hour. This proves especially relevant for scenarios like a customer finishing an evening shift at 11 PM who needs to apply for a mortgage, a request that would traditionally wait until business hours. The same logic applies to insurance claims and other customer service inquiries that benefit from round-the-clock availability. The underlying principle is that AI should remove friction from customer interactions rather than simply digitize existing processes, and that convenience should not be constrained by organizational operating hours.

Smit recognizes that the proliferation of Large Language Models has fundamentally altered what consumers expect from financial service providers. Daily exposure to conversational AI in other contexts has raised the bar for interactions with banks and insurers. This shift makes AI adoption a business imperative rather than an optional strategic decision, though Smit maintains a nuanced view of competitive dynamics — she argues that AI technology alone won't sustain long-term advantage, and that at most, early adopters benefit from a temporary first-mover window. The implication for organizations is that AI capability must be accompanied by other differentiating factors such as customer service quality, product innovation, and brand trust.

The most distinctive element of NN Group's AI strategy lies in its people-first orientation. The company has rolled out an AI literacy initiative encompassing all 16,000 employees, a commitment that mirrors large-scale enterprise training programs but distinguishes itself through its connect to individual role evolution. Critically, the training extends beyond teaching staff how to operate AI tools; it connects the technology to NN Group's broader transformation strategy and addresses what the shift means for each employee's individual role — how they can contribute and how their responsibilities may evolve. This approach recognizes that successful AI adoption requires employees to understand not just the mechanics of new tools but their place within the organization's evolving mission.

This emphasis on human readiness reflects Smit's foundational belief that technological success hinges not on the technology itself but on how employees engage with it. The principle of lifelong learning has existed within NN Group's culture for years, but artificial intelligence has dramatically accelerated its urgency. An unexpected driver of internal demand for training has been the widespread media coverage of AI developments, which has raised public awareness and, in turn, generated strong employee interest in developing AI literacy. This organic demand suggests that the organization's investment in AI education aligns with broader societal interest in the technology, potentially making adoption smoother than in organizations where training must be mandated rather than embraced.
