## 500-word summary

Digital transformation has become critical for small and medium-sized enterprises as customers increasingly seek cheaper, faster, and more sustainable alternatives. This practical guide explores how SMEs can initiate transformation and why it proves essential for survival and growth in today's competitive landscape. Many companies lack the knowledge of how to begin this change, but a proper digital transformation strategy can prevent frustration and keep projects on track.

Digital transformation revolves around creating an agile and future-proof organization centered on the end user—both customer and employee. The goal is to add value to products, services, customer contact, and brand experience. Technology and digitalization serve as tools within a continuous process encompassing business innovation, social innovation, and technological innovation. Importantly, digitization and digital transformation represent different concepts. Digitization focuses on making business processes and data available digitally, enabling easier connection of information, data enrichment, and comparison. These insights then fuel innovation, meaning digitization functions as one component of broader digital transformation.

This transformation requires a fundamental shift in the organization, placing the end user at the center of the entire customer journey. It develops through a digital strategy and vision, enabled by people and technology. Current working methods, processes, business operations, and the ICT landscape need overhauling—not just experimentation with new technologies.

For successful digital transformation, SMEs should start with their business strategy using models like BHAG, the Ashridge model, or Business Model Canvas. This involves mapping the business model, including the vision for the future and tomorrow's customer journey, identifying which trends and developments impact the business, where opportunities lie, and what unmet customer needs exist. Leaders should consider how to optimize the customer journey and contact, validating plans with employees through internal validation and with customers through external validation. This upfront validation assesses whether ideas create added value for end users and remain viable and technically feasible before investing. New insights then refine the plans, establishing the strategic starting point.

The human factor represents perhaps the most important element of digital transformation. People need to work and think differently. Leadership, a clear vision and strategy, and ample room for social innovation ensure sustainable behavioral change at every organizational level. Actively involving employees and customers proves crucial—developing innovations through co-creation and testing with target groups maximizes relevance. Explaining the how, what, and why in accessible language matters significantly. Traditional organizations rely on top-down communication, but successful transformation requires open dialogue. Involving a cross-section of the organization in important decisions provides greater insight and anticipates potential pitfalls. Simultaneously, giving employees a voice creates unity and ownership. These employees become ambassadors who understand the new strategy and communicate it to colleagues, creating a ripple effect that spreads throughout the organization and builds awareness around common goals.
