## 500-word summary

For Dutch companies weighing AI investments, the decision to hire an internal AI lead or engage an external partner is both strategic and expensive. The article argues that getting it right builds momentum and capability, while getting it wrong can waste six months on hiring delays, unclear ownership, or external work that never internalizes. In the Netherlands, AI adoption is accelerating: CBS reports 22.7% of companies used AI in 2024, with adoption rising sharply among mid-sized firms (50-250 employees). However, talent shortages complicate the equation—ManpowerGroup’s 2026 survey shows 73% of Dutch employers struggle to fill vacancies, with AI skills growing in importance. PwC’s 2025 workforce findings add that only 24% of employees are satisfied with employer-provided AI training. Against this backdrop, the article presents a framework for decision-making.

An internal AI lead is best suited when AI is becoming a lasting operating capability, leadership is aligned, the role is concrete with defined outcomes, and there is sufficient internal infrastructure (data, product, engineering, executive access). The role requires more than technical expertise—it demands credibility across business, product, operations, and governance. This choice is weak when leadership cannot define the first 3-5 use cases, ownership is politically unclear, or the company needs a roadmap more than a hire.

An external AI partner is stronger when speed is critical, use cases are not yet prioritized, internal ownership is still emerging, and the company wants to test before committing to a permanent hire. External partners can deliver readiness assessments, use-case discovery, roadmap design, governance setup, vendor due diligence, pilot design, and executive workshops. The risk is dependency, so the best partners help build internal capability. AI Coalition 4 NL frames 2026 as a shift from isolated pilots to broader business integration, reinforcing the need for structured approaches.

The article strongly recommends a staged hybrid path for most Dutch SMEs: Phase 1 involves an external partner for an AI readiness assessment and a 90-day roadmap; Phase 2 uses that partner to achieve first wins and define the internal role; Phase 3 hires the internal AI lead with a clear mandate; Phase 4 transitions the partner to an advisory role. This reduces two key risks: hiring the wrong role too early and outsourcing too long.

Seven diagnostic questions help companies decide: What business problem? Are first 3-5 use cases known? Who is executive sponsor? Do we need diagnosis, ownership, or delivery? How long would hiring take? Is the role clearly defined? How to avoid external dependency?
