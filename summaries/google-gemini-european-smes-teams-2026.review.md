## 500-word summary

Google released Gemini 2.0 at the start of this year, and for European operations teams it represents the most consequential AI platform decision of the decade. For companies already running on Google Workspace, Gemini is not a separate tool—it is the AI layer that activates across Gmail, Docs, Sheets, and Meet without requiring a new vendor relationship, separate data pipeline, or six-month procurement process. This integration advantage is particularly significant for growing software teams and mid-sized companies already paying for Workspace licences. The current Gemini line includes two primary models relevant for SME teams. Gemini 2.0 Flash is the faster, lower-cost model handling document summarisation, email drafting, meeting transcription, and code assistance with low latency—making it the right default for operations teams running high-volume, repetitive tasks. Gemini 2.0 Pro is the higher-capability model handling complex reasoning, longer documents, and multi-step analysis, and powers Gemini Advanced, Google's subscription tier for users needing the full capability set. These models are accessible through two separate surfaces: Gemini for Google Workspace (the embedded assistant experience) and Vertex AI (the enterprise API platform for custom integrations). Conflating these is one of the most common mistakes operations leaders make. Gemini for Workspace embeds AI assistance directly into tools teams already use—in Gmail it drafts, summarises, and classifies email threads; in Docs it generates, rewrites, and formats content; in Sheets it interprets data, writes formulas, and produces plain-language summaries; in Meet it transcribes calls, generates action items, and produces meeting notes automatically. For a mid-sized company without a dedicated AI engineering team, this is the most practical entry point with no API configuration, prompt engineering, or model selection decisions required. For GDPR compliance, Google offers an EU data region for Workspace ensuring data at rest and in use stays within EU boundaries, plus a Data Processing Amendment addressing GDPR Article 28 processor obligations including data subject rights, breach notification timelines, sub-processor disclosures, and data deletion commitments. Vertex AI requires separate configuration at the project level specifying Google Cloud region. Practical use cases with clear ROI for 10-50 person teams include meeting documentation eliminating administrative burden for teams running 15-20 calls weekly, document drafting reducing blank-page friction in proposals and reports, spreadsheet interpretation allowing non-technical team members to produce formulas from plain language, and email triage reducing response time for high-volume inboxes. Gemini's core advantage over Microsoft Copilot is Workspace integration depth and pricing flexibility at smaller scales; over standalone Claude or ChatGPT subscriptions it's the lack of tool-switching. However, platform-embedded AI means being bound to Google's product roadmap—teams wanting model flexibility will need alternatives. The key strategic question for European operations leaders is whether the integration convenience outweighs the loss of vendor independence, particularly as AI capabilities continue evolving rapidly and Google's pace of model releases may not align with every organisation's timeline.
