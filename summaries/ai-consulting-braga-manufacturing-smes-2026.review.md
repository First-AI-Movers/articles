This article examines AI adoption for manufacturing SMEs in Braga, Portugal, a region with a concentrated cluster of automotive components, electronics assembly, and textile production suppliers operating as Tier 2 or Tier 3 manufacturers. These businesses produce components to precise specifications and deliver on customer-determined schedules, operating under ISO certification with disciplined, documentation-heavy processes. The article argues that broad AI transformation programmes are impractical for 40-person component manufacturers; instead, businesses should identify the two or three processes where AI reduces cost or error rate, pilot those, and integrate them into existing quality management systems. The article identifies three productive first use cases. First, quality control documentation: AI assists in drafting non-conformance reports in required formats and translating documentation for German, French, and Dutch customers, though every AI-assisted document requires quality manager review before release. Second, supplier and customer communication: AI tools with multilingual capability help production managers draft communications in customer languages and summarize incoming documentation, though this remains AI-assisted first draft rather than automation. Third, production scheduling analysis: AI helps identify scheduling conflicts earlier, flag orders with tight lead times, and surface patterns in late deliveries, though integration with ERP systems like SAP, Primavera, or PHC represents the bottleneck. The article recommends a fractional CTO engagement model for companies with 20 to 60 employees, typically running two to four days per month. This covers use case prioritization to identify applications with the shortest path to measurable outcomes, vendor assessment for EU data residency and GDPR compliance, pilot design with defined success criteria, and governance setup documenting how AI tools are used and incorporated into ISO quality systems. Regarding EU AI Act compliance, most manufacturing AI use cases are not high-risk and do not affect health, safety, fundamental rights, or consequential individual decisions. Two exceptions apply: AI used in safety-critical process control may qualify as high-risk, and AI used in employment decisions falls under Annex III requiring conformity assessment. For standard operational AI, manufacturers need data processing agreements with vendors, a record of tools in use, and basic internal policies covering appropriate use and review requirements. The article notes that documentation-drafting pilots using commercial AI tools typically cost EUR 100-300 monthly, while ERP integration projects typically cost EUR 5,000-20,000 depending on system complexity. Industry associations such as COTEC Portugal and CIP provide resources and occasionally run subsidized AI adoption programmes, with EU funding through Portugal 2030 available for SME digitalization. The article emphasizes that AI serves as a drafting and analysis tool requiring human oversight rather than an autonomous approval system, allowing manufacturers to capture efficiency gains while maintaining quality control discipline.
