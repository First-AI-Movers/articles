ABN AMRO Bank has successfully deployed over 25 generative AI use cases in production, targeting a cost-to-income ratio below 50% by 2028. While this achievement is notable, the article uses it as a contrast to highlight a troubling pattern: many enterprises invest heavily in transformation initiatives without achieving sustainable results. The core issue, identified as 'The Execution Delusion,' is a systematic failure where organizations announce ambitious visions, hire consultants, evaluate platforms, and launch pilots, only to encounter harsh realities. These include inadequate data quality, processes that exist only as undocumented tribal knowledge, decision-making that lacks clear ownership, and ROI projections that collapse under scrutiny. The fundamental error is solving technology problems when the real challenge is organizational readiness.

To address this, the article proposes a Value Protocol that begins not with frameworks or technology platforms, but with three foundational activities. First, Decision Architecture Mapping requires documenting who makes decisions, what information they need, and what constraints they operate under. The article specifies that if this cannot be completed within two weeks, it signals that the organization is not ready for transformation. Second, Data Quality Triage involves evaluating data specifically for decision-usefulness rather than completeness. The article notes that organizations typically discover that 70% of their data provides minimal decision-making value, implying that most data efforts are misdirected. Third, the Process Documentation Reality Check insists that if critical processes exist only in employees' heads rather than in documented procedures, transformation failure becomes inevitable. These three steps are designed to uncover the actual readiness gaps before any technology is selected.

The article also offers an immediate tactical action: the 7-Day Challenge, which involves conducting a Decision Flow Audit. This audit selects one critical business process, maps each decision point, identifies required data inputs, assigns decision ownership, and tracks the duration of decisions. The article emphasizes that this exercise should require no more than eight hours; if it takes longer, it indicates why current transformation approaches continue to fail. This direct, time-boxed audit forces organizations to confront their process and data deficiencies head-on.

The implications for leadership are clear. Rather than rushing to adopt generative AI tools, C-suites must first ensure that their organization has decision-ready data, clearly documented processes, and unambiguous ownership of decisions. The article warns that without these fundamentals, even well-funded AI initiatives will likely produce minimal returns. The ABN AMRO example shows that successful deployment is possible, but only when organizational readiness precedes technology investment. The Value Protocol and Decision Flow Audit provide a concrete, low-risk way to test readiness before committing substantial resources. By addressing the Execution Delusion, leaders can avoid the pattern of high spending with low impact and instead build a foundation for sustainable AI-driven transformation.
