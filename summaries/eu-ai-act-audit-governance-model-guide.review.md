## 500-word summary

For leaders at growing Dutch companies using AI, the EU AI Act presents a strategic decision that goes beyond compliance: what level of intervention actually matches their organization's maturity? The article identifies three distinct interventions - compliance audits, governance setups, and full AI operating models - and explains why buying the wrong one creates legal, operational, and reputational debt. The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, with prohibited practices and AI literacy obligations taking effect February 2, 2025. General-purpose AI model rules applied from August 2, 2025, while high-risk systems face an August 2, 2026 implementation deadline. Some AI embedded in regulated products has until August 2, 2027. A compliance audit serves companies lacking basic visibility into their AI usage. It produces four outputs: an inventory of AI systems, first-pass risk classification, a gap view against current obligations, and a prioritized remediation list. This is the right choice when teams have adopted tools informally and leadership needs a baseline understanding of exposure. However, an audit alone does not create ongoing decision-making capacity. A governance setup addresses the next layer - establishing ownership, AI policies, intake and approval processes, model and vendor registers, documentation standards, human oversight expectations, incident escalation paths, and role-based AI literacy training. This is recommended when AI use is expanding across multiple teams and the business needs repeatable decision processes rather than one-time findings. The Dutch market is already full of governance-focused services indicating this is where many companies should focus in 2026. A full AI operating model combines governance with prioritization, use-case selection, delivery management, data and systems integration, vendor strategy, training, adoption metrics, change management, and leadership reporting. This is necessary when AI touches several business functions, is embedded in customer-facing products, or spans multiple vendors and internal builds. At this stage, governance alone is too thin - companies need disciplined execution across the business. The author warns that most companies buy too low or too high: they either purchase a one-off audit and assume they're done, or jump into transformation before establishing basics. The recommended sequence for most growing companies is audit first for visibility, then governance for structure, then operating model for execution discipline. However, the path isn't always linear - some firms can combine stages one and two, while others need the operating model sooner if AI is already embedded across operations or product. The core message is simple: match the intervention level to the actual problem. A beautiful audit report doesn't create ownership, a policy document doesn't create adoption, and a transformation program doesn't magically fix missing controls.
