## 500-word summary

The CEO Playbook for the First 90 Days of AI Adoption provides a practical framework for business leaders navigating AI implementation without creating organizational chaos. The guide emphasizes that CEOs should not attempt to become the most technical person in the company but rather focus on strategic leadership responsibilities: defining why the business is adopting AI, establishing ownership structures, determining acceptable risks, and identifying the first controlled move. The playbook divides the 90-day journey into three distinct phases designed to build alignment progressively.

Days one through thirty concentrate on narrowing the problem by identifying where the business experiences the most repetitive friction, which decisions are slow or costly, whether AI is genuinely the right lever, and who should own the initiative internally. The critical instruction is to avoid starting by purchasing tools before defining the business problem worth solving. Leaders must resist the common temptation to evaluate technology solutions before articulating what specific business challenge they intend to solve.

Days thirty-one through sixty shift to assessing readiness and constraints by reviewing workflow stability, data and system limitations, governance expectations, leadership bandwidth, and establishing review and escalation paths. This phase determines whether the organization should proceed with implementation or wait until readiness improves. The assessment covers technical infrastructure, data quality and accessibility, existing governance frameworks, and whether the leadership team has sufficient capacity to support a new initiative alongside current responsibilities.

The final phase, days sixty-one through ninety, explicitly rejects the goal of scaling in favor of a controlled first move, which may include a tightly scoped workflow experiment, a consulting engagement to sharpen priorities, targeted team training tied to specific workflow changes, or a deliberate decision to wait. The framework emphasizes that the objective is not to achieve scale by day 90 but rather to establish a foundation for future expansion.

The CEO's personal responsibilities include owning the organizational reason for AI adoption, managing ambition level and budget discipline, serving as the decision-maker when trade-offs appear, and establishing clear standards for measuring success. These leadership behaviors distinguish successful AI transformations from those that stall or fail.

The playbook identifies five common failure patterns to avoid: tool-first buying, unclear ownership between technology and operations, pilots with no decision criteria, innovation work disconnected from business priorities, and training that creates awareness without changing behavior. Each pattern represents a predictable pitfall that derails AI initiatives.

At the day-90 checkpoint, leadership must be able to answer five questions: what business problem is being solved first, who owns the work, what is the next scoped move, what are the main risks, and whether consulting, readiness work, or implementation support is needed next. If these answers remain vague, the organization should not attempt to scale.
