Direct cost savings represent the most intuitive category—reducing time spent on document drafting, email handling, research, or report formatting. Three CFO-ready metrics anchor credible business cases. Time-to-value (30-60 days for most operational AI tools) sets realistic payback period expectations. Cost per outcome translates subscription costs into defensible per-unit figures (such as 10 EUR per contract review assist). Productivity baseline shift captures the before-and-after hourly equivalent—demonstrating, for example, that reducing an 8-hour weekly task to 3 hours across a four-person team at 55 EUR per hour yields 57,200 EUR in annual productivity gains. Two critical mistakes undermine AI business cases. First, comparing AI costs against headcount triggers political resistance and is usually arithmetically flawed, since AI rarely eliminates entire roles. The correct approach compares against outcome costs—what it costs to produce an output today versus with AI assistance.
