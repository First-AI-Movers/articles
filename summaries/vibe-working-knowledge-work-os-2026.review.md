## 500-word summary

Vibe working is Anthropic's term for a fundamental shift in knowledge work that matters more than faster answers or better prompts. The core change is moving from treating AI as a tool you consult—asking questions, pasting outputs, tweaking, and repeating—toward treating AI as a team you manage. Where vibe coding was about describing a feature and letting AI write the code, vibe working is about describing a business outcome and letting AI coordinate the work. The critical difference is not the underlying model but the workflow itself. Anthropic shipped three ingredients that make this real: agent teams that orchestrate multiple Claude Code sessions in parallel, Claude embedded directly inside PowerPoint and spreadsheets (eliminating the copy-paste tax that breaks formatting and loses half your time to glue work), and a 1M-token context window in beta on their developer platform that allows large projects to stay coherent rather than getting chopped into fragments through chunking, RAG, and summarization pipelines that lose nuance. This transforms knowledge work from serial task execution—where you act as the router, sending individual prompts to an AI intern—into management of parallel agent teams. You define the outcome, provide constraints and quality bars, delegate to specialized agents (researcher, analyst, writer, builder), then review, correct, approve, and ship. The practical implications are massive: one person can now manage a small swarm of specialized digital workers, changing throughput for competitive analysis, product discovery synthesis, security questionnaires, due diligence, customer research summaries, roadmap option decks, incident retrospectives, procurement comparisons, internal enablement docs, and executive briefs. The article recommends three immediate actions: rewriting prompts as outcomes with acceptance criteria rather than task requests, building a small agent org chart with four starting roles, and converting repeatable work into systematic skills run like pipelines. The key risk is that vibe working does not replace the best people—it replaces those who never upgraded from simple prompting, because once you can orchestrate parallel agents, the bottleneck becomes taste, judgment, domain knowledge, quality control, and decision-making. That is management, not typing. The article is written in a practical, leadership-oriented voice and emphasizes that this shift requires humans to upgrade their skills from prompt crafting to outcome definition and team orchestration. The three enabling technologies that make vibe working viable are already available: agent orchestration for parallel execution, embedded AI in everyday productivity tools, and the massive context window that keeps complex work coherent. This represents the most significant change to knowledge work productivity since the spreadsheet, and leaders who adopt this operating system early will compound their advantage over those who treat AI as a faster typewriter.
