## 500-word summary

This article presents a practical six-month fractional CTO roadmap designed specifically for founder-led European SMEs struggling with AI adoption. The central thesis is that these companies do not fail at AI because they lack ambition or willingness, but because no single person owns the technical decisions required to move from evaluation to implementation. The solution is a structured fractional CTO engagement that makes explicit who decides what, by when, and how success gets measured across six months.

The roadmap divides into three distinct phases, each with defined deliverables and founder decision points. The first two months focus on audit and foundation: the fractional CTO conducts a comprehensive tool inventory to map existing AI spend, usage, and shadow IT, performs a risk assessment covering GDPR compliance gaps and EU AI Act classifications for systems affecting hiring, credit decisions, or critical infrastructure, and produces a 90-day priority list ranked by business value and implementation readiness. The founder's decision at this stage is which business processes to prioritize for AI intervention, a choice only they can make given their knowledge of customer impact, regulatory exposure, and strategic direction.

Months three and four concentrate on pilot execution with specific selection criteria: processes must have a measurable baseline, an internal champion, and a realistic six-to-eight week implementation window. The fractional CTO configures and tests tools with actual team members rather than in sandbox environments, because integration problems surface only under real usage. The output is a pilot report documenting measured ROI such as time saved, error reduction, or revenue cycle improvement, along with at least one procurement decision. The founder's decision is budget approval for production tooling, ensuring organizational buy-in before scaling.

The final phase converts successful pilots into team-wide capability through rollout, training, documentation, and governance. The fractional CTO produces a team AI playbook covering tool usage, constraints, and escalation procedures, establishes a three-person governance committee (typically founder, operational lead, and fractional CTO), and configures a metrics dashboard for ongoing performance measurement. The most consequential founder decision is whether to extend the engagement or hand off to internal leadership, which depends on the team's built AI capability and whether the roadmap uncovered use cases requiring deeper technical leadership.

The engagement structure typically runs one to two days per week (eight to sixteen hours) at EUR 2,500-4,500 monthly, totaling EUR 15,000-27,000 for the initial six-month term. The article emphasizes this differs fundamentally from one-off AI consultants because the fractional CTO owns accountability for what the team can do independently when the engagement ends, not just the deliverables handed over. Extension beyond six months makes sense when the audit revealed a second tier of high-value processes, the company faces a significant regulatory event like an acquisition or market entry, or no internal candidate has the technical depth to own governance independently. However, the article warns that extension should not be a default; a fractional CTO engagement that cannot articulate a clear handoff plan by month five has a structural problem more time will not fix.
