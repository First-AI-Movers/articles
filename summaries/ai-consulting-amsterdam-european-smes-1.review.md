The article challenges the prevailing assumption that AI consulting means wholesale digital transformation, arguing instead that most small and medium enterprises benefit from focused, scoped engagements that address specific business questions rather than comprehensive organizational overhauls.

Effective AI consulting should enhance decision quality, narrow project scope, and enable leadership to proceed methodically. For most European SMEs, consulting becomes valuable when executives need assistance addressing fundamental questions: which business problems warrant immediate attention, which workflows represent realistic candidates for AI integration, whether a readiness assessment should precede broader initiatives, and who should assume internal ownership of AI adoption. The article emphasizes that consulting engagements should produce clear, actionable outputs rather than abstract strategic visions that lack implementation pathways.

The piece cautions Amsterdam buyers to distinguish between visibility and actual fit, recommending they seek consulting partners who prioritize business outcomes over technical demonstrations, maintain clarity regarding scope and deliverables, demonstrate realistic awareness of governance requirements and team capacities, and possess the integrity to advise against premature scaling efforts. The critical differentiator is whether a consultant is willing to say no to certain requests and steer clients away from initiatives that are not yet viable for their specific organizational context.

Regarding timing, AI consulting typically represents an appropriate initial step when leadership already recognizes AI's significance but requires guidance on strategic direction and implementation sequencing. This commonly involves narrowing use case options, achieving alignment among CEO, CTO, and operations leadership, prioritizing investment areas, and determining whether to pursue readiness building, training, or implementation support. The article stresses that consulting adds the most value during the strategic clarification phase rather than during technical implementation.

Conversely, organizations lacking clear executive ownership, the ability to identify one or two meaningful workflows, or operating with undefined experimental boundaries—or those whose fundamental challenge stems from weak process discipline rather than AI strategy—should pursue narrower assessment phases rather than comprehensive consulting mandates. In these cases, the underlying organizational foundation is not yet ready to absorb consulting recommendations effectively.

The article concludes with a recommended sequential approach for SME leaders: first define the business problem, then confirm internal ownership, identify the most credible initial use case, assess readiness and operational risk, and finally determine whether expanded consulting engagement is warranted.
