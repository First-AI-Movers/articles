The core argument centers on the unsexy truth that transformation vendors won't tell executives: before touching any AI framework, organizations need to map their decision flows—not data flows or process flows, but decision flows. This is the boring prerequisite everyone skips, and it's why they fail. When companies implement transformation initiatives, they consistently discover that their data quality is atrocious, their processes exist only as tribal knowledge, decision-making is distributed chaos, and nobody owns the outcomes. The expensive mistake is attempting to transform without first understanding what organizations are changing from. The author notes that the most frequent questions he receives point to this fundamental misunderstanding, with executives asking about AI frameworks, debating automation platforms, and comparing transformation methodologies—all wrong questions that miss the organizational readiness issue entirely.

The article outlines three prerequisites for transformation success that successful companies adopt rather than starting with technology. First, Decision Architecture Mapping requires documenting who makes what decisions, with what information, under what constraints—ideally accomplished in under two weeks, and if this cannot be done, the organization is not ready to transform anything. Second, Data Quality Triage involves auditing data not for completeness but for decision-usefulness, with most organizations discovering that 70% of their data is noise that doesn't support actual decision-making.
