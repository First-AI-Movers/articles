The article details what a real assessment should include across six dimensions: business priorities (starting with what the company is trying to improve such as margin, speed, quality, or compliance), workflow analysis (identifying repetitive work, slow decisions, context loss, and broken handoffs), data and systems reality (assessing data accessibility, cleanliness, and integration friction), governance and risk (clarifying use case sensitivity, human review needs, and missing policies), adoption capacity (determining ownership, manager alignment, team trust, and training requirements), and economics with prioritization (producing a shortlist of use cases with value potential, complexity, dependencies, risk level, owners, and recommended sequence). The author warns against three common mistakes SMEs make: tool-first (buying Copilot or ChatGPT Enterprise before understanding value), demo-first (running workshops and collecting ideas without prioritizing against business impact), and vendor-first (letting sellers define the problem to match their product). The article specifies five concrete outputs a real assessment should produce: a clear current-state view, a prioritized use-case list (not dozens of ideas), a blocker map identifying what will stop progress, a practical roadmap for the next 30/60/90 days, and a decision on operating model (consultant, delivery partner, internal enablement, or combination). Red flags to identify weak assessments include: lead magnet quizzes that cannot guide investment decisions, maturity scores without next-step sequences, providers who ignore workflows, governance treated as a footer rather than core, and assessments conducted without business, operations, technical, and governance stakeholders in the room. For SMEs, the recommended involvement includes one business decision-maker, one operations or workflow owner, one technical or data lead, one risk or compliance voice, and one adoption driver. The article concludes that most Dutch SMEs need four things before spending on AI: a sober baseline, a shortlist of real use cases, a view of blockers, and a practical next-step roadmap.
