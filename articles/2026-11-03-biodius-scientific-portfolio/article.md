> **TL;DR:** The public Biodius evidence in one place: a technical preprint (not peer reviewed), two case pages, three editorials, the offer, the FAQ and the limits.

**This page is one path through the public scientific material behind the Biodius Target Challenge, and it says what each item is.** There is one technical preprint, two case pages drawn from it, three editorial articles, the Biodius Target Challenge offer and its FAQ. The limits come before the offer.

Why this matters: a biotech team or investor deciding whether to trust this work needs to separate our own preprint from commentary and from a commercial offer.

## How to read the labels

- **Technical preprint.** Our own report, deposited on Zenodo. It is not peer reviewed and it is not a journal paper.
- **Technical report, web edition.** The same work on this site. Where it and the preprint differ, the preprint is the reference.
- **Case page.** An account of one experiment from the preprint, taken from our own prospectively frozen analysis of public data.
- **Editorial analysis.** Articles by First AI Movers that explain a method or a question. They are not peer-reviewed publications. Where they use an example, they say it is hypothetical.
- **Offer and FAQ.** A description of a service. It is not evidence for the method.

## The technical preprint (version 1.1)

**Falsification-First AI for Biological Target Assessment: Lessons from Four Prospectively Frozen Experiments.** Costa, H. (2026). Preprint v1.1, 1 October 2026, on Zenodo. Open access under CC BY 4.0. The preprint's own status line reads "Technical Report / Preprint. Not peer reviewed."

- Cite all versions with the concept DOI: [doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23057911](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23057911). This is the identifier our other pages use.
- This version has its own DOI: [doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23100159](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23100159). The record page is [zenodo.org/records/23100159](https://zenodo.org/records/23100159).

### What it reports

Four experiments, each bounded in advance, using public data and no new laboratory work:

1. A strong agentic research baseline recovered known biology better than a heavier architecture and matched it on four frozen discipline dimensions. One evaluator judged one domain, and a third arm was not run.
2. A later, smaller safeguard layer preserved its specified safeguards in one fresh-domain transfer. That is one transfer.
3. An ulcerative colitis causal-chain study ended as not evaluable on available public data.
4. A TTPAL challenge: one gut-resident T-cell discovery resource supported TTPAL in 3 of 66 designated groups, with PP4 up to about 0.992. The primary independent blood cohort showed no usable signal, with PP4 at most 0.031 across the six measured blood T-cell classes.

## Two case pages from the preprint

### Ulcerative colitis causal chain (H3): not evaluable

A biologically coherent ulcerative colitis hypothesis survived an initial evidence bar and two adversarial AI reviewers. A frozen human-genetics adjudication then found that the decisive causal link could not be evaluated from lawful public data. The hypothesis was not promoted, and it was not disproven. Read [Not Evaluable Is a Result](https://radar.firstaimovers.com/not-evaluable-is-a-result).

**Limit:** the result describes what available public data can decide. It does not say the causal chain is false.

### TTPAL: a striking signal that did not replicate

An unexpected T-cell signal reached PP4 up to about 0.992 in three gut-resident groups in a discovery dataset. The three groups overlap within one donor lineage, so they are not three independent replications. It did not reproduce in independent public blood T-cell cohorts. Read [0.992 Was Not Replication](https://radar.firstaimovers.com/pp4-0992-was-not-replication).

**Limit:** the discovery cells were gut-resident and the independent cohorts were blood. The result does not prove a tissue-resident effect false. It shows the signal was single-dataset-only. PP4 is a model score, not the probability that TTPAL causes disease.

These are editorial analysis written for decision-makers. They are not peer-reviewed publications and they present no new experimental results.

- [How Much Evidence Is Enough Before a Biotech Decision?](https://radar.firstaimovers.com/how-much-evidence-is-enough-biotech-decision) How to choose a review scope around the decision, with a map of search coverage, study independence and remaining uncertainty. ## Limitations

These come from the preprint and from the pages above.

- **The preprint is not peer reviewed.** It is our own report and has not been independently replicated. The method has not been externally validated.
- **Small scope.** Four experiments in two disease areas, using public data only. The results support a narrow claim about workflow design, not a general claim about AI in biology.
- **No laboratory work.** Nothing was tested experimentally.
- **Tissue mismatch.** In the TTPAL challenge, the independent cohorts were peripheral blood and the discovery signal came from gut-resident cells.
- **Narrow architecture comparison.** One evaluator, one domain, and a third arm that was not run. It does not rank all research architectures.
- **AI reviewers are not experts.** They generate challenges that were then adjudicated against primary sources. They do not replace qualified scientific judgment.
- **Interest declared.** The preprint states that its author is affiliated with Hernani Holding B.V., which owns the Biodius research software compared as the heavier architecture in the first experiment.
- **Editorial pieces are not research.** The three articles are editorial analysis. They use hypothetical examples where stated.
- **No clinical or therapeutic claim.** Nothing here establishes a therapeutic target, a clinical mechanism, a diagnostic, or the safety or efficacy of any intervention. Biodius does not provide clinical advice, validate safety or efficacy, perform wet-lab work, or replace legal/patent/regulatory review.
- **No discovery or prediction claim.** Biodius does not discover drug targets or predict clinical success. It is experimental scientific diligence tooling, and our current commercial hypothesis is that it is useful before a costly downstream decision.
